QUESTION: How much is the fee when a member overdraws their checking account?

Consumer Fee Schedule | Overdraft and returned item fees
Source: consumer-fee-schedule.html
Version 8 (current). Replaces Version 7. Owner: Member Services.

When Larkfield pays a check, debit card purchase, or automatic payment that overdraws a member's checking account, the overdraft fee is $29 per item paid. When Larkfield returns an item unpaid because the account does not have enough money, the returned item fee is $29 per item returned. No more than three overdraft or returned item fees are charged to one account in a single business day.
No overdraft fee is charged if the account is overdrawn by $10 or less at the end of the business day.
Members can link a savings account for overdraft transfers. An overdraft transfer from a linked savings account is free.

ANSWER
There is a conflict between the two versions of the Consumer Fee Schedule:

- **Version 8 (current)** [consumer-fee-schedule.html]: The overdraft fee is **$29 per item paid**, and the returned item fee is **$29 per item returned**. No more than **three** overdraft or returned item fees are charged to one account per business day. Additionally, in this version, no overdraft fee is charged if the account is overdrawn by $10 or less at the end of the business day.

- **Version 7** [consumer-fee-schedule-v7.html]: The overdraft fee is **$35 per item paid**, and the returned item fee is **$35 per item returned**. No more than **four** such fees are charged per business day. There is no mention of a de minimis overdraft threshold in this version.

Both versions note that an overdraft transfer from a linked savings account is free [consumer-fee-schedule.html][consumer-fee-schedule-v7.html].

Since Version 8 explicitly states it is current and replaces Version 7, the $29 fee, three-fee daily cap, and $10 de minimis exception appear to be the applicable current terms [consumer-fee-schedule.html]. However, both documents are labeled "(current)" in the source metadata, so this discrepancy should be flagged rather than resolved unilaterally.
